# Turn the example into your own pilot

The demo supplies the mechanics. Your business supplies the customer evidence, product and permission to act. Start with one product and one path to a buyer.

| Decision | Work with your agent | Keep as evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Is the problem real? | Describe a specific customer's task; separate observed pain from your hypothesis | Dated research notes with permission to retain them |
| Is the product useful? | Build one inspectable sample; ask a fresh reviewer to use it | Sample, instructions and reviewer findings |
| Is the offer clear? | State buyer, deliverable, exclusions and price hypothesis | Reviewed offer and unresolved questions |
| Can a buyer receive it? | Select owner-controlled checkout and delivery; run in provider test mode | Test receipt, delivered asset, duplicate-event and failure checks |
| Can you support it? | Draft delivery, refund and escalation procedures | Named human decision owner and a rehearsed support case |
| Can you reach buyers legitimately? | Choose one permitted channel and one useful contribution | Exact approved scope and destination readback |
| Did the pilot work? | Reconcile live orders, refunds, fees and costs over a stated window | Source-backed outcome, uncertainty and next decision |

## Your first task

Fill the company brief, then write one task. A good first task is small enough to finish locally: “Draft a one-page offer for this buyer using these research notes. Preserve the open questions. Stop after the draft and review it against these criteria.”

A weak task is “Launch the company and make revenue.” It leaves the agent to invent the audience, product, permissions and evidence.

## When to move beyond local work

The business owner chooses the accounts and explicitly approves the first external action. Confirm the correct destination and how you will verify success before acting. Treat provider test mode as test evidence. When a mutation may have succeeded, inspect the destination before retrying.

Automate a recurring step only after its manual path works and its failure handling has been exercised. The repo's Python checks validate a local teaching example; they do not control an agent's external tools. Use [the production limits](limitations.md) to identify the controls your actual integration needs.
